Reruns: two directions, one recommendation

The current app, Direction A ("tinted desk") and Direction B ("sessions"), built as swaps of the real Svelte components and rendered from one library whose cards, Steam art and footage agree. A, tinted desk
Same layout, refined: warm umber surfaces, recessed navigation, box art at its own 2:3 shape, a tinted game header, a raised Play key, moments in their own timeline lane.
B, sessions
The library reads as evenings of play: a game band from the game's own art, clips grouped by logged play session, the day's games on All clips, one diamond for a saved moment everywhere, Set as cover while paused, the session's other clips under the viewer.

Recommendation: B

B is the one that could only be Reruns. A is better-made than the current app, but put side by side it is the same app in a warmer light. B changes what the library is about, using only facts Reruns already records. These are the decisions that do it:

  1. The diamond is the saved moment. ◆ where you pressed F7, ◇ where F8 saved the clip. It appears under the card's picture, in its own lane on the timeline (never on top of a frame, where the old white flags looked like the playhead), and as "2 marked" in the transport. Video editors already read a diamond as "a point in time worth keeping".
  2. Sessions, not files. A game page groups clips by the play sessions the recorder logs ("Yesterday 9:05 – 11:20 PM, 2h 15m played"). All clips stays chronological by day but says what you played that day. Nothing is invented: a clip outside a logged session falls back to its day. Grouping by day remains in the page's menu.
  3. Game colour, bounded. The game's own key art and logo, in a 156 px band (120 px in short windows, 108 px with no Steam art), tinted from that art. The tint also marks the selected game row and the session hairlines, and nothing else: the navigation, controls and orange stay the app's own. The page keeps a full row of clips in view at 900 × 600.
  4. Art composed, not cropped. Steam's 2:3 box art at its own shape in the navigation, the viewer header and next to a card's game, instead of a square cut from the top of a portrait.
  5. You never lose your place. The card's picture flies into the player and back to the card of the clip you ended on (even after ← →), which rings once. In the viewer, the rest of that session's clips sit in the spare room under the controls, one click each, only when the player keeps its full size.

Worth taking from A into B: the raised Play key and the top light on Copy clip. Not A's warm surfaces: in B the chrome stays neutral so the game's colour is the only one that changes.

This brief asks for things the last design brief banned. B's band fades the art into its tint with a mask-image gradient, and game colour now reaches the chrome (DESIGN.md and accents.ts said it never would). If the fade reads as the gradient you rejected before, the fallback is a hard-edged, framed art panel; the rest of B doesn't depend on it.

Recommended release: "the saved moment"

  1. Time after the press (off / 5 / 10 s) with the press recorded in the sidecar, shown as ◇ on the timeline.
  2. Set as cover, in the sidecar, on T and while paused.
  3. A game's page by play session, with the compact game band; day grouping stays in its menu.
  4. Copy from a card follows the Sharing limit, so copy-then-paste into Discord always works.
  5. Direction B's visuals around those, plus A's Play key.

Each is small, none touches the capture path except the wait after the press, and together they make the release about one thing: the moment you saved. The longer, disk-backed replay is the next release (parity with Steam, but in the capture path). Not on the default player: audio cleanup, highlights, phone transfer.

Real, prototype, untested

Real: the app does this today

  • Replay clips, F7 marks in sidecars (carried into edits), Steam capsule, hero and logo art from Steam's cache, thumbnails that keep the clip's shape.
  • Play sessions logged per game with start and end; 50 newest per game in the library listing.
  • Copy clip in the viewer rendering trim, mix, 9:16 and slow motion to fit the Sharing size, with progress, Cancel, Copied, Try again.

Prototype: runs in the pictures, not built for real

  • Cover frame: drawn from the playing video into memory, gone on reload. Real: sidecar + thumbnailer.
  • Art tint: read from the image in the page, which needs CORS. The driver serves it; Tauri's asset protocol may not, and then it falls back to the hand-picked colour. Real: compute in Rust at listing.
  • Data plumbing: games and clips reach the library and viewer through a module store written by the navigation, so App.svelte is untouched; the viewer opens a session's clip through the library. Real: props from App.
  • ◇ Saved here sits at the clip's end, true for an untrimmed replay today; it only earns its place with time after the press.
  • Session times in the pictures are fixture data shaped like sessions.jsonl; clips, art and footage are real.
  • Open/return continuity, the session strip, the band, the diamonds: built and exercised in Edge (the WebView2 engine), not in the app window.

Untested

  • Linux (WebKitGTK): none of this has run there. Watch mask-image on the band, OffscreenCanvas for the tint, the flight's smoothness, color-mix() and oklch() on older WebKitGTK.
  • Inside Tauri at all: tint extraction, crossorigin on the player video (the media server allows it for the waveform), drag-out from cards with the new markup.
  • Logos whose own colours sit badly on their tint; very bright key art behind the search field.
  • Libraries over 120 clips: the grid then renders only nearby rows, so after stepping far with ← → the return finds no card to land on and just doesn't fly.
  • The flying picture is the 320 px thumbnail scaled up to the player for ~240 ms; soft, on screen briefly. Judged acceptable in frames, not on a real display.
  • Whether pasting HEVC or AV1 files copied as they are embeds in Discord (Copy sends the original codec when nothing changed).
